# MTG Master - Commander Deck Analysis, Deckbuilding and Remote Play

**MTG Master** helps **Magic: The Gathering Commander (EDH)** players understand how their decks actually play.

It is an independent platform for building, managing, analyzing, tuning, sharing and playing Commander decks with more clarity.

MTG Master reviews practical deck signals such as power level, Commander bracket fit, mana consistency, ramp, card draw, interaction, protection, combos, win conditions, opening hands, collection fit and Rule Zero expectations.

The platform is built around a simple idea:

> Commander decks are systems, not just card lists.

The goal is not to replace player creativity or experience. The goal is to help players make better deckbuilding decisions, have clearer Rule Zero conversations and enjoy better games.

## MTG Master in One Sentence

MTG Master is a Commander-focused deck analysis platform that helps players evaluate power level, bracket fit, mana, ramp, draw, interaction, combos, opening hands, collection options and table expectations.

## What MTG Master Is

MTG Master is a tool-first platform for Commander players.

It is designed to help answer practical questions like:

- Is this Commander deck consistent enough?
- What Commander bracket or power level does this deck fit?
- Does the mana base actually support the deck?
- Can the deck cast its commander reliably?
- Do I have enough ramp, draw, removal and protection?
- What is the real game plan?
- Are the win conditions clear?
- Are the combos intentional or incidental?
- What should I improve first?
- Which cards from my collection can improve this deck?
- What cards should I keep in mind for future upgrades?
- Can I scan and organize my physical cards more easily?
- Can I test or play this deck with friends online?

Instead of only storing a decklist, MTG Master focuses on **how and why a Commander deck works**.

## How MTG Master Is Different

Many Magic tools focus on card search, decklists or popularity data.

MTG Master focuses on **Commander deck understanding**.

It helps players look at a deck as a complete system:

- How the mana base supports the plan
- Whether ramp appears early enough
- Whether card draw is reliable
- Whether interaction is sufficient
- Whether the commander is essential or optional
- Whether win conditions are realistic
- Whether combos change the expected table experience
- Whether the deck fits its intended bracket
- Whether the deck can be explained clearly before a game

MTG Master is especially useful when players want to move beyond “this deck is probably a 7” and talk about real table expectations.

## Who MTG Master Is For

MTG Master is built for players who want to:

- Build and manage Commander decks
- Analyze deck structure and consistency
- Understand Commander brackets and power expectations
- Organize their physical Magic card collection
- Scan cards instead of entering everything manually
- Track cards they want to buy, trade for or test later
- Improve existing decks without rebuilding everything from scratch
- Upgrade preconstructed Commander decks with a clear plan
- Understand mana, ramp, draw, interaction, combos and win conditions
- Compare decks against real table expectations
- Share decks publicly or privately
- Play manual Commander games online with friends

It is useful for casual players, upgraded-precon players, high-power casual pods, experienced brewers, collection-heavy players and groups who want clearer language around deck strength and table fit.

## Core Features

### Commander Deck Management

MTG Master lets players create, save, organize and manage Commander decks.

Decks can be kept private or shared publicly, depending on how the player wants to use them.

### Commander Deck Analysis

MTG Master analyzes Commander decks beyond the raw card list.

It looks at:

- Mana base structure
- Ramp package
- Card draw and advantage engines
- Removal and interaction
- Protection
- Win conditions
- Combo presence
- Mana curve and tempo
- Early, mid and late-game plans
- Commander synergy and dependency
- Structural weaknesses and pressure points

The focus is practical: helping players understand what their deck is doing well and where it may fail in real games.

### Commander Bracket and Power-Level Awareness

Commander power level is often unclear and subjective.

MTG Master uses structured Commander bracket thinking to help players evaluate deck strength in a more consistent way.

It helps players think about:

- Casual and precon-level decks
- Upgraded Commander decks
- Optimized casual decks
- High-power decks
- cEDH-oriented builds
- Game speed
- Combo pressure
- Tutor density
- Card quality
- Table fit

The goal is not to force a perfect label. The goal is to support better Rule Zero conversations and reduce mismatched games.

### Mana and Opening Hand Evaluation

MTG Master includes tools to review how reliable a deck is in the early turns.

This includes:

- Mana source evaluation
- Color access
- Ramp timing
- Opening hand quality
- Early-turn consistency
- Commander castability
- Common failure patterns

This helps players understand whether a deck only looks good on paper or can actually start games reliably.

### Combo Detection

MTG Master helps identify combos and synergy packages inside Commander decks.

This makes it easier to understand:

- Which combos exist in the list
- Whether they are central or incidental
- How they affect power level
- Whether they match the intended table experience
- Whether they should be mentioned during Rule Zero

### Rule Zero and Table Fit

Commander is a social format. A deck is not only defined by its cards, but also by the experience it creates at the table.

MTG Master helps players think about:

- Expected deck strength
- Game speed
- Combo presence
- Interaction density
- Stax or lock pieces
- Commander dependency
- Win condition clarity
- Whether the deck matches the intended playgroup

This makes it easier to explain a deck before a game and avoid mismatched expectations.

### Collection Management

MTG Master includes collection management tools for players who want to organize their physical Magic cards and use them during deckbuilding.

The collection system helps players:

- Track owned cards
- Organize cards by quantity, set, language, foil status or other details
- Connect owned cards with deckbuilding decisions
- See whether a deck uses cards already in the collection
- Identify missing cards
- Find realistic upgrades from cards the player already owns
- Make better use of bulk, binders and stored cards

The goal is to make the collection useful, not just stored.

For Commander players, this matters because many deck upgrades are not about buying the most expensive staple. Often, the right card is already sitting in a binder or box.

### Card Scanner

MTG Master includes a card scanner to help players add physical cards to their collection more quickly.

The scanner is designed to reduce manual entry and make it easier to work with real paper collections.

It can help with:

- Scanning physical Magic cards
- Adding cards to the collection
- Reducing manual search and entry
- Handling collection bulk more efficiently
- Supporting faster deckbuilding from owned cards

The card scanner is part of the wider goal of connecting physical cards, collection management and deckbuilding into one workflow.

### Wishboard

MTG Master includes a Wishboard for cards players want to track outside their current collection or decklists.

The Wishboard is useful for:

- Cards to buy later
- Cards to trade for
- Cards to test in a deck
- Upgrade ideas
- Side ideas that are not ready for the main deck yet
- Future deckbuilding plans

Instead of losing card ideas in notes, chats, screenshots or browser tabs, players can keep them connected to their MTG Master workflow.

The Wishboard helps separate:

- Cards already owned
- Cards currently used in decks
- Cards missing from a deck
- Cards being considered for future upgrades

This makes deck planning and collection growth easier to manage over time.

### Collection-Aware Optimization

MTG Master can take the player’s owned cards and Wishboard into account when thinking about deck improvements.

This supports more realistic upgrade paths:

- Prefer cards already owned when they solve the problem
- Highlight missing cards when they are important
- Keep possible upgrades visible for later
- Help players decide what is worth buying, trading for or testing
- Prioritize impact and role coverage over raw card price

The result is a more practical tuning process, especially for players who build from real collections rather than unlimited card access.

### Pauper Commander and PDH Support

MTG Master also supports Pauper Commander and PDH workflows.

PDH support is focused on the specific structure of the format, including uncommon commanders and common-card deck construction.

### Remote Commander Table

MTG Master includes a **Remote Commander Table** feature for playing manual Commander games online with friends.

Remote Table is designed for synchronized, player-controlled games where MTG Master manages the shared table state while players remain in control of the rules and decisions.

Current Remote Table focus:

- Private Commander rooms
- Deck selection from saved decks
- Player zones
- Battlefield, graveyard, exile, command zone, hand and library handling
- Life totals
- Commander tracking
- Counters and basic game actions
- Room-based multiplayer play
- Manual play with synchronized state

Remote Table is currently in beta and is designed primarily for desktop and laptop screens.

## Design Philosophy

MTG Master is guided by a few principles.

### Context over popularity

Cards matter because of how they function in a specific deck, not only because they are commonly played.

### Clarity over mystery

Players should understand why a deck receives a certain analysis or recommendation.

### Consistency over goldfish results

A deck should be evaluated by how reliably it executes its plan in real games.

### Player agency over automation

MTG Master should support player decisions, not replace them.

### Collection reality matters

Deckbuilding happens with real cards, real budgets, real binders and real trade-offs.

### Table experience matters

Commander is social. Power level, expectations, budget, archetype identity and playgroup context all matter.

## What MTG Master Is Not

MTG Master is not meant to be a one-click deck replacement tool.

It is not designed to remove creativity from deckbuilding or tell every player to build the same optimized list.

It is also not just a decklist storage site.

The purpose of MTG Master is to help players understand decks better, use their collections better, make smarter changes and play better games.

## AI Use and Limitations

MTG Master uses AI-assisted analysis to help explain Commander decks, detect structural issues and suggest improvements.

AI output should be treated as guidance, not as official rules advice or a replacement for player judgment.

MTG Master is designed to support better decision-making, but Magic is complex and Commander is highly contextual. Deck strength, table fit and play experience depend on playgroup expectations, pilot skill, local meta, budget, sequencing, threat assessment and Rule Zero conversations.

Players should always use their own judgment.
